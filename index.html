The bet we're making

At £2 to £3 a copy, this can't be won by selling books one at a time through ads. It's won by putting the book in front of groups that buy in tens and hundreds, libraries, universities, schools, churches, leadership and mentoring programmes, and by getting you onto platforms where one interview reaches thousands of readers at once. Ads have a job here, but it's to point warm traffic at the page and the coverage, not to chase strangers.

Who I've done this for

I'm currently working with Michael Vecchione on Hard Guys Cry. I've also worked on several of his earlier books, handling pre order announcements, signed copy messaging, bookstore callouts, reader focused copy and website traffic. Alongside that I've worked with Teresa Michael and B.G. Howard, and built trailers and social campaigns for authors, gaming and business clients. More of it is at philipoladele.com.

How the work unfolds

Three clear phases

Phase one, before 14 November

A landing page built for the Kidderminster launch, with your story, the charity pledge, buy links and an email signup. Meta Pixel and Google Analytics installed so we see what's working from day one. A book trailer cut for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube. Email capture set up, since there's no list today. Amplify the Kidderminster event before, during and after. Turn the Cameroon radio interview, the New York writer and The Herald coverage into a run of posts rather than one off announcements. Phase three, after launch

Weekly pitching to podcasts, radio and publications, logged in one tracker so you see every pitch and response. Library, university and school submissions, with a pack prepared for each. A reader review push on Amazon and Goodreads. A monthly report on reach, where readers are, email growth, media features, submissions and sales wherever tracking allows.

Why £2,400 and not more

At your net per copy, £2,400 is roughly 800 books. I could ask for more, but then the book has to sell itself out of a hole it can't. What this fee actually buys is the institutional doors and the media features you listed as your measures, and those are worth far more to you than the copies are.

The honest part about ads

I haven't built ad money into either fee. You fund the ad budget directly, capped at £200 a month, and I don't touch it. Over three months that's £600 of ads, roughly 200 books at your net per copy, so we keep it pointed at warm traffic and stop anything that isn't earning its place. I'd rather show you that number than bury it in a package.

What I commit to

The landing page live within two weeks of starting, the trailer within three, at least 12 pitches to podcasts, radio and publications in the first month, and a written update every Friday, no exceptions.

How we measure it

Using your own list: Amazon and physical sales, reach and where it's coming from, library and university submissions, reader reviews, and the number and reach of media features across print, online, radio, TV and podcast.
